Theme: I Am Your Mirror
Sub-Theme/Chapter: Androgynous Tailoring and the Masculine-Feminine Mirror
==================================================
**Candidate elements/concepts**

1. Structural Foundations | Split-Lapel Deconstructed Tuxedo | 30%
The centerpiece of the "Architectural Double," this jacket features a sharp, 1940s-inspired shoulder line that creates a powerful inverted triangle silhouette. One half of the peak lapel is crafted in a luminous Silk Satin while the other remains in a matte Wool Gabardine, creating a physical manifestation of the shadow self. The color is a light-absorbing Obsidian Black, providing a stark canvas for the play of textures. This construction emphasizes the tension between light and dark, femininity and masculinity, and the high-society history of Givenchy’s formal evening wear while maintaining a gender-neutral appeal through its rigid, non-waisted drape.

1. Structural Foundations | High-Waisted Architectural Wide-Leg Trousers
Drawing from the 1930s dandyism of Annemarie Schwarzenbach, these trousers sit exceptionally high on the waist, cinched to create a dramatic contrast with the pooling fabric at the hem. The cut is generous and architectural, utilizing Flint Gray wool to provide a neutral, stony tone that suggests strength and resilience. Deep pleats at the front allow for fluid movement that challenges the rigid lines of traditional tailoring, ensuring the garment moves like a "sartorial ghost" around the body.

2. Reflective Surface Accents | Mercury-Finish Leather Belting | 25%
Functioning as literal mirrors within the ensemble, these belts are crafted from ultra-high-shine, silver-toned calfskin leather. The finish mimics the appearance of liquid mercury, designed to catch and bounce the high-contrast runway lighting. The hardware is minimal and concealed to allow the reflective surface to remain the uninterrupted focus. Positioned at the natural high waist, these accessories bridge the gap between structured jackets and voluminous trousers, serving as a focal point of visual tension and a literal interpretation of the "Mirror" theme.

2. Reflective Surface Accents | Polished Silver "Mirror" Buttons
Small but critical, these buttons are made from solid polished steel with a mirror-chrome finish. They are used sparingly on the oversized "Bettina" cuffs and the front closures of trench-capes. The silver-white brilliance of the metal contrasts sharply against Obsidian Black wool, acting as tiny reflective points that rhythmically catch the light, suggesting the "Double" through repetitive, symmetrical placement.

3. The Intellectual Nomad | Floor-Length Heavy Wool Trench-Capes | 20%
A tribute to the traveler’s spirit, these capes merge the utility of 1930s travel gear with the elegance of a Givenchy couture gown. Fabricated in dense Wool Gabardine in a stark, blinding Optical White, they provide a dramatic, spectral presence. The silhouette is expansive and floor-sweeping, designed to be draped over deconstructed tailoring to add layers of mystery. The functional elements—such as oversized internal pockets and a high, stiffened collar—ground the ethereal color in a sense of utilitarian chic.

4. Heritage Reimagined | Stiffened Oversized "Bettina" Cuffs | 25%
The iconic Bettina shirt is evolved through the lens of masculine rigidity. These cuffs are exaggerated in scale and heavily stiffened to maintain a razor-sharp, geometric edge, designed to protrude significantly from the sleeves of tuxedo jackets. They are executed in a crisp Cotton Poplin in a brilliant, clinical Optical White. The high-contrast white against Obsidian Black tailoring creates a graphic, rhythmic visual that pays homage to Hubert de Givenchy’s original 1952 design while aligning with the theme’s focus on the erasure of gendered construction.

4. Heritage Reimagined | Elongated Silk Faille Waistcoats
Designed to be worn against bare skin, these waistcoats extend past the hip to elongate the torso, mimicking the proportions of 1930s masculine evening dress. They are constructed from Silk Faille in a deep Midnight Blue-Black, offering a subtle tonal variation from the Obsidian Black jackets. The texture is ribbed and structured, providing a "hard" feminine layer that reflects the light differently than the surrounding wool, further exploring the concept of the self found in the "other."
