Theme: I Am Your Mirror
Sub-Theme/Chapter: Androgynous Fluidity — The Borrowed Frame
==================================================
**Candidate elements/concepts**

1. Tailored Precision | The 'Apex' Structured Shoulder | 25%
This element reimagines the traditional 1930s menswear shoulder for a contemporary feminine frame. The construction utilizes internal horsehair canvassing and subtle padding to create a sharp, slightly extended horizontal line that tapers into a narrow waist. This architectural "frame" serves as the primary structural anchor for the collection's blazers and tunics. Functionally, it provides a sense of authority and presence, crafted from crisp wool mohair and heavy silk radzimir. The color palette is dominated by Midnight Navy and Ink Black, providing a deep, light-absorbing surface that emphasizes the garment’s silhouette.

1. Tailored Precision | The 'Oxford Bag' High-Rise Trouser
Drawing inspiration from the voluminous trousers of the interwar period, these bottoms feature an ultra-high waist and an expansive leg width that creates a dramatic, floor-sweeping verticality. The trousers include deep, inward-facing pleats and a sharp permanent crease to maintain a linear, mirrored symmetry. The fabric choice of Slate Grey tropical wool ensures the garment has enough movement to mimic liquid when in motion, yet enough weight to hold its structural shape when standing.

2. Functional Symbolism | The Integrated 'Cinch' Utility Belt | 15%
To bridge the gap between nomadic utility and couture precision, this key element features a wide, polished calfskin belt integrated directly into the waistbands of coats and trousers. The belt functions as both a structural cincher and a nod to the safari-influenced travel gear of the 1930s. It features multiple minimalist loops and a hidden magnetic fastening system for a sleek, futuristic finish. The material is a high-shine Onyx Black leather, creating a sharp textural contrast against the matte textures of the wool garments.

2. Functional Symbolism | The Detachable 'Officer' Stiff Collar
This element serves as a direct reference to Annemarie Schwarzenbach’s rebellious adoption of masculine formalwear. The collars are executed in multi-layered, starched cotton poplin, providing a rigid, sculptural frame for the neck. These are detachable, allowing for a transition from a severe, high-neck "officer" aesthetic to a more relaxed, open-collar look. The color is strictly Optic White, acting as a bright, reflective highlight against the darker tones of the outer layers.

3. Outerwear Reflections | The 'Shadow' Bonded Wool Trench | 20%
This critical outerwear piece explores the concept of the "double" through its proportions and weight. The trench coat is elongated to hit just above the ankle, featuring oversized lapels and storm flaps that are bonded rather than stitched to create a seamless, monolithic appearance. The bonding technique provides a paper-like stiffness that holds its shape even when unbelted. In shades of Charcoal and Graphite, the coat is designed to cast a long, cinematic shadow, mimicking the high-contrast aesthetic of 1930s Film Noir.

4. Refined Finishes | Mirrored Nickel Hardware | 10%
As a literal interpretation of the "Mirror" theme, all closures, including buttons, eyelets, and belt buckles, are finished in a high-shine, liquid-effect polished nickel. These elements act as small, reflective beacons on the garments, catching the light and breaking up the monochromatic discipline of the fabrics. The hardware is minimalist in design—smooth, circular, and devoid of visible branding—emphasizing the intellectual minimalism of the collection.

4. Refined Finishes | Elongated French Cuffs
The shirts in this chapter feature exaggerated, stiffened French cuffs that extend past the knuckles, requiring the wearer to fold them back or wear them as a dramatic extension of the arm. This detail adds to the "Borrowed Frame" concept, suggesting a garment that is slightly oversized yet perfectly tailored. Materials include heavy silk crepe de chine and stiff cotton, primarily in Optic White and pale Silver-Grey tones to draw attention to the hands and gestures of the wearer.
