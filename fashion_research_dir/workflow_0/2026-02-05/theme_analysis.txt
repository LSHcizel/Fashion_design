**Theme:**
Une Lettre d’amour (A Love Letter) is a collection that reimagines the Givenchy archive through the lens of romantic correspondence and the intimacy of the written word. It explores the tension between the private vulnerability of a confession and the public elegance of the Givenchy silhouette. This theme serves as a poetic bridge between Hubert de Givenchy’s historic devotion to his muses and a modern, fluid interpretation of love that transcends gender and time. The collection is framed as a sensory experience, where the rustle of silk mimics the unfolding of parchment and the sharp lines of tailoring represent the precision of a fountain pen’s stroke.

**Inspiration:**
The primary inspiration for this collection is the life and aesthetic of Annemarie Schwarzenbach, a 1930s Swiss writer, photographer, and traveler known for her strikingly androgynous style and restless spirit. Her literary pursuits and documented correspondences provide a narrative of "longing from a distance," which translates into a collection that balances travel-ready utilitarianism with high-society sophistication. By integrating Schwarzenbach’s "tomboy" elegance—characterized by wide-leg trousers and structured shirting—with Givenchy’s signature "Bettina" romanticism, the collection creates a dialogue between the masculine and feminine. The "I Am Your Mirror" motif is woven throughout, suggesting that a love letter is not just an address to another, but a reflection of the writer’s own soul and identity.

**Key aspects of the theme:**
**Epistolary Tailoring:**
This aspect focuses on the structural translation of a letter into garment construction. Shoulders are sharp and precise, echoing the corners of an envelope, while lapels and plackets feature asymmetrical folds that suggest the act of sealing a secret. The use of heavy wools and stiff silks provides a "paper-like" quality to the outerwear, ensuring that each piece maintains a sculptural integrity that feels both protective and formal, reminiscent of Hubert de Givenchy’s mid-century masterpieces.

**The Ink Palette and Monochromatic Sentimentality:**
Drawing from the visual language of a manuscript, the color story is dominated by "Inkwell Black," "Parchment Cream," and "Sepia." These are punctuated by "Oxblood Red," symbolizing the wax seal that secures a private thought. The monochromatic focus emphasizes texture and form over pattern, allowing the quality of the craftsmanship to speak to the "purity of intent" found in a handwritten note. This palette reinforces the brand’s DNA of aristocratic restraint while adding a layer of moody, intellectual depth.

**Androgynous Correspondence:**
Inspired by Annemarie Schwarzenbach’s defiance of traditional gender norms, this aspect merges menswear and womenswear codes. Oversized trench coats and silk neckties are paired with delicate lace inserts and sheer paneling. This juxtaposition represents the duality of the theme: the strength required to express love and the softness of the emotion itself. By blurring these lines, the collection honors Givenchy’s history of dressing iconic, independent women who possessed a self-assured, often unconventional, grace.

**Tactile Script and Materiality:**
The theme is physically manifested through intricate textile work that mimics the act of writing. Jacques Doucet-inspired embroidery and jacquards feature abstract, cursive lines that flow across the body like spilled ink. Delicate organza is layered to create a "vellum" effect, offering glimpses of the skin beneath, suggesting the transparency and honesty required in a love letter. Hardware, such as buttons and clasps, is designed to resemble nibs and vintage writing instruments, grounding the ethereal theme in tangible, luxurious detail.
