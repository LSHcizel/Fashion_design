**Chapter 1 (Sub-Theme): Emotional intimacy**

*   **Concept:**
    This opening chapter introduces Une Lettre d’amour as a private act of dressing: garments conceived like correspondence kept close to the skin. Emotional intimacy is expressed through proximity, softness, and hiddenness—clothes that feel read rather than seen. The chapter begins the collection with a sense of confession: refined, quiet, and deeply personal, as though the first pages of a love letter are being unfolded in a hushed room. Givenchy’s discipline frames the vulnerability, ensuring the emotion remains controlled, elegant, and never sentimental. The result is a wardrobe of closeness—pieces that cradle the body, reveal only in fragments, and carry the tactile memory of being touched, folded, and treasured.

*   **The Looks:**
    *   **Silhouette:**
        The silhouette stays close to the body, elongated and precise, with a gentle tension between tailoring and softness. Jackets are slim and slightly nipped at the waist, shirts are fluid but contained, and dresses skim rather than expose. Long lines create a sense of composure, while subtle wrapping and draped closures suggest the protective gesture of enclosing a letter. The chapter should feel intimate in scale, as if each look has been designed for one recipient only.

    *   **Garments:**
        Tailored coats with concealed fastening, silk blouses with scarf ties, column dresses with inner overlays, and narrow trousers anchor the chapter. A few evening pieces introduce sheer inserts, bias-cut slips, and wrapped bodices that hint at vulnerability without spectacle. Details such as cuffed sleeves, folded lapels, and lining peeking at the hem evoke the act of opening an envelope or revealing a handwritten note. The wardrobe should feel layered but restrained, with masculine elements softened through drape and touch.

    *   **Fabric & Color:**
        The palette is intimate and paper-like: ivory, parchment, blush, powdered rose, warm grey, and ink black. Deep burgundy appears sparingly as a sealing-wax accent, bringing emotional weight. Fabrics emphasize tactility and closeness—silk crepe, satin-backed crepe, fine wool, soft leather, organza, and delicate lace used with precision rather than excess. Matte and sheen should be carefully balanced, reflecting the emotional interplay of concealment and revelation.

*   **Ambiance:**
    *   **Lighting:**
        Lighting should be low, warm, and directional, like late afternoon light across a desk where a letter is being read. Soft shadows and a quiet glow around the body create an atmosphere of privacy and reflection. The runway space should feel enclosed, almost like an inner room, reinforcing the sense that the collection is a personal message.

    *   **Music:**
        The music should be sparse and intimate: a restrained piano line, distant strings, and subtle breath-like textures. The rhythm must be slow and suspended, allowing silence to feel as important as sound. The soundtrack should evoke solitude, anticipation, and the tender pause before a confession is made.

*   **Analysis:**
    This chapter functions as the emotional key to the entire collection, establishing the language of closeness that will later expand into memory, tension, and departure. By grounding Une Lettre d’amour in intimacy, the chapter makes the theme tactile and human—fashion not as display, but as contact. It also introduces the collection’s central duality: masculine structure softened by feminine tenderness. In Givenchy terms, it affirms that elegance can be deeply personal, and that restraint can intensify emotion rather than diminish it.
