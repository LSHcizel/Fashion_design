**Theme:**
Sous le Salon la Plage is a Chanel Cruise narrative of origin and release: a return to Biarritz, where the house’s modern identity first took shape, and a reimagining of salon codes as they flow toward the sea. The collection frames liberation as a physical and social movement—between couture and workwear, black dress and swimwear, structure and ease—while preserving the house’s signature discipline, elegance, and craft.

**Inspiration:**
Matthieu Blazy’s Cruise 2027 for Chanel draws directly from Biarritz as both a historical birthplace and a personal place of freedom. The show’s first runway presentation in the coastal city, staged at Le Casino Municipal, activates the memory of Gabrielle Chanel founding her first couture house at Villa Larralde in 1915 and building a new female identity rooted in independence, seaside leisure, and social mobility. The collection also revisits key Chanel myths and codes: the meeting with Ernest Beaux and the N°5 lineage, the 1926 black dress as a radical modern classic, and the house’s early transformation of working-woman dress into aspirational luxury. Blazy translates these foundations into a contemporary language of striped seaside graphics, elevated workwear, swimwear, and architectural detailing, turning Biarritz into a symbol of absolute freedom and Chanel’s enduring ability to make rebellion look inevitable.

**Key aspects of the theme:**
**Heritage as living origin:**  
The collection is anchored in Biarritz not as a nostalgic backdrop but as Chanel’s first design laboratory for modernity. Heritage here functions as an active source of invention, where the founder’s entrepreneurial leap, the city’s Atlantic atmosphere, and the house’s early liberation of dress codes all become design fuel.

**Freedom as bodily movement:**  
The theme centers on the body in motion—dressing, undressing, swimming, walking, and crossing settings without costume change. Silhouettes and styling suggest a woman who owns her rhythm, with garments designed to move from salon refinement to shoreline ease and from formal poise to spontaneous relaxation.

**Rebellion refined into classics:**  
Blazy emphasizes that Chanel’s classics were originally disruptive. The 1926 black dress, reinterpreted through the bow detail, and the elevation of workwear staples reveal a design logic where subversion is polished into timelessness rather than softened into conformity.

**Salon-to-beach duality:**  
A core tension runs through the collection between the codes of interior elegance and outdoor freedom. The couture mindset is not abandoned at the shore; instead, it is relaxed, softened, and made fluid, producing a wardrobe that feels equally at home in a grand interior, on a casino terrace, or in the sun.

**Workwear re-authored as luxury:**  
Marinière stripes, bleu de travail references, and washed canvas tailoring shift class associations while retaining practical credibility. This aspect gives the collection its modern credibility, showing how utility can be transformed into a refined, high-craft luxury language without losing its functional spirit.

**Coastal graphic identity:**  
Basque stripes, marine shimmer, shell references, and seaside color associations create a strong visual vocabulary that is not literal resort dressing. The graphics and surfaces evoke the Atlantic world through rhythm, repetition, and tactile brightness, allowing the collection to feel coastal without becoming costume.

**Craft, structure, and surface tension:**  
The collection’s materials—tweed, raffia, silk, beaded knitwear, sequins, canvas—create a dialogue between softness and precision. The reworked Double C as a structural element reinforces Chanel’s architectural intelligence, while shimmering and tactile surfaces suggest the sea, light, and movement.

**Accessories as passage tools:**  
Bags, shoes, and jewelry are conceived as instruments for moving across environments and identities. From waterproof flaps and striped beach paniers to Art Deco heels, barefoot heel plates, shell earrings, and pearls, the finishing pieces extend the theme of freedom by making transition itself look elegant.

**Sub-themes for chapter development:**
**1. Origin Point, Recast as Motion**  
This chapter can explore Biarritz as the birthplace of Chanel’s modern liberation, translating the house’s historical breakthrough into garments that feel mobile, confident, and newly unbound. It should merge archival references with contemporary seaside energy, using black, stripes, and sculptural details to show how a classic is born from movement rather than stillness.

**2. The Wardrobe That Crosses the Shore**  
This chapter can develop the salon-to-beach continuum through workwear, swimwear, and high-craft resort tailoring. The narrative should focus on adaptable elegance: pieces that shift between social codes and landscapes, balancing utility, sensuality, and craftsmanship in a way that makes freedom feel wearable.
