Look 4: Crossing Layers | Shirt-Dress Over Swim

1. The Outerwear: Striped Shirt-Dress Cover-Up
A straight, lightly oversized shirt-dress falls in crisp Basque stripes of navy and white, its cotton shirting softened enough to move with sea air but still holding a clean architectural line. The body is cut with a sharp collar, dropped shoulder, and cuffed long sleeves that can be pushed up and stay in place, while the front placket runs in precise buttons so it can be worn fully open, halfway closed, or belted into a dress. The hem lands just below the knee, making it versatile enough to read as both a refined cover-up and a day dress. Worn over the swim layer, it creates a clear salon-to-shore transition: the structure at the top frames the body, then the fabric loosens as it descends, allowing glimpses of the silhouette beneath and reinforcing the feeling of effortless passage from interior polish to the waterline.
    *   Key Structure: sharp collar; straight oversized cut; cuffed sleeves; knee-skimming length
    *   Material & Finish: fine cotton shirting; matte, breathable surface; pearlized buttons and restrained tonal stitching

2. The Foundation: Swim Layer and Streamlined Base
Beneath the shirt-dress sits a clean swimsuit-based bodysuit in deep navy with a high, softly squared neckline, functioning as the look’s disciplined understructure. The line is sculpted but not tight, with minimal seams and a smooth leg finish so it can disappear under the striped layer or appear intentionally at the opening of the placket and hem. A narrow shoulder and precise armhole keep the upper body neat, while the fit through the torso supports movement without compression. This foundation gives the look its practical credibility: it allows the wearer to go from terrace to shoreline without changing identity, and its dark, matte surface provides a calm counterpoint to the crisp graphic stripe above.
    *   Top: navy swim bodysuit with a high, clean neckline and minimal seam construction
    *   Bottom: integrated brief line with a smooth, moderate-cut leg opening
    *   Fit & Line: close to the body but comfortable; streamlined through the torso to preserve a long, uninterrupted vertical

3. The Details: Materiality & Hardware
Small Chanel-like refinements keep the look precise without interrupting its ease. The shirt-dress carries tiny white buttons with a faint pearl glow, echoing the sea light caught on the stripes, while the cuffs and side seams are finished with nearly invisible topstitching that emphasizes the garment’s clean construction. A slim self-belt in the same navy-and-white shirting, lightly interfaced so it ties neatly at the waist, adds a practical point of definition without over-controlling the silhouette. The belt can be knotted softly over the open dress or used to close it into a more polished daytime shape, creating a visual hinge between relaxed movement and tailored intention.
    *   Hardware Focus: small pearl-finish buttons; discreet side placket; minimal tonal stitching
    *   Waist Treatment: narrow self-belt in matching stripe fabric, lightly structured for a neat wrap and subtle waist definition

4. The Finish: Footwear & Stance
The look finishes with flat leather sandals in salt white or deep navy, shaped with a clean, low vamp and slim ankle strap so the foot remains light and close to the ground. Their minimal construction keeps the vertical line of the shirt-dress intact while lending practical stability for walking across stone, deck, or sand. The stance they create is relaxed but assured: the wearer appears ready to move, with the hem swaying around the knee and the open edges of the shirt-dress shifting gently against the swim base. The sandals complete the crossing mood by grounding the look in ease rather than display, making the final impression one of controlled freedom.
    *   Shoe: flat leather sandals with slim straps, low profile, and a refined matte finish in salt white or navy
