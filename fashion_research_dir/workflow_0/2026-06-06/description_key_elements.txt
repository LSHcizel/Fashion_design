- Heritage & provenance:
  - Chanel’s foundational Atlantic-coast heritage in Biarritz, tied to Gabrielle Chanel’s first couture house at Villa Larralde in 1915.
  - The location as a turning point for financial independence, social mobility, and the birth of Chanel’s liberated women’s silhouette.
  - Historical links to Ernest Beaux and the origin story of Chanel N°5.
  - Matthieu Blazy’s personal affinity with the Basque Country and his framing of Biarritz as “absolute freedom.”

- Show / location context:
  - First-ever Chanel runway presentation in Biarritz, staged at Le Casino Municipal.
  - A setting that fuses seaside leisure, grand hotel glamour, casino society, and the emotional weight of maison history.
  - A coastal runway atmosphere where salon refinement is released into beach freedom.

- Creative director intent:
  - Matthieu Blazy’s return to Chanel’s origin point through a contemporary lens.
  - Reasserting that classics can emerge from rebellion.
  - Aligning natural world and modern world; loosening rigid dress codes.
  - Celebrating dressing and undressing as a rhythm of freedom and pleasure.

- Silhouette & garment vocabulary:
  - Tribute to the 1926 black dress with a back bow detail translated into accessories.
  - Clean lines, fluid dresses, unbound silhouettes, and sporty elegance.
  - French workwear classics elevated to luxury: marinière stripe shirt, bleu de travail jacket, washed cotton-canvas tailoring.
  - Swimwear as a key wardrobe node; silk scarf sets, raffia skirts, supple tweed, beaded knitwear, and sequin surface pieces.

- Materials, craft & surface:
  - High-craft construction across casual and formal codes.
  - Washed cotton-canvas, supple tweed, soft beaded knitwear, raffia, silk, sequins evoking fish scales.
  - Architectural use of the Double C as garment structure rather than mere logo placement.
  - References to marine shimmer, tactile movement, and artisanal polish.

- Color, stripe & graphic codes:
  - Strong Basque stripe language threaded through the collection.
  - Black as a foundational Chanel code, reactivated through the 1926 dress reference.
  - Coastal, nautical, and marine graphic cues rather than literal resort imagery.
  - Blue-collar and seaside color associations implied through workwear and water references.

- Accessories & finishing:
  - Compact luggage, waterproof flap bags, oversized striped beach paniers, and Basque pala carry bags.
  - Footwear spanning Art Deco heels to barefoot “heel plates,” enabling movement between ballroom and sand.
  - Shell earrings and a return to Chanel pearls as a spiritual motif.
  - The bow motif reappearing as an accessory translation of archival detail.

- Mood, liberation & wearer fantasy:
  - Freedom, ease, rebellion, and bodily rhythm.
  - A woman moving across settings without changing identity: salon, beach, ballroom, and workwear.
  - Sensual practicality, sunlit confidence, and class-crossing modernity.
  - A “mermaid fantasy” balanced by disciplined elegance and historical self-possession.
