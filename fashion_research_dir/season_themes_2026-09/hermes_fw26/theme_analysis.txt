**Theme:**
Hermès Fall/Winter 2026 is titled Entre chien et loup, the hour when a dog cannot be told from a wolf. Twilight is a state of dress: romance and utility occupy the same garment, and the woman decides how much of either to show.

**Inspiration:**
Nadège Vanhée set the Paris show at dusk and invoked Hecate in the notes, then kept the clothes muscular rather than mythological. Leather leads. Zips run diagonally or the full length of a dress so a garment can be opened, closed, and remade through the day. Equestrian codes appear as jodhpurs, flat riding boots, aviator jackets, and riding coats, then harden into urban pieces such as glossy lambskin cycling shorts. Color moves the way a sunset does: sulfur yellow, oxblood, forest green, iridescent burgundy, then inky blue and charcoal. A later Los Angeles chapter of the same season turns the Carré into a dress and borrows ballet, a dancer leaving rehearsal with a jacket thrown on.

**Key aspects of the theme:**
**Twilight as calibration:**
Night is not simply black. Surfaces change in the light. The palette is emotional, and the silhouette stays narrow, aerodynamic, and exact.

**Utility that can open:**
Zips, pockets, and adjustable openings are decorative because they work. A zip-front mini can reveal another shirt. The same piece serves more than one hour.

**Leather as muscle:**
Ostrich becomes structure: jumpsuits, long dresses, shells that behave like musculature. Sheepskin collars, trenches, and parkas protect. Craft here is directional, not nostalgic.

**The Carré, worn:**
In the Los Angeles chapter, the house scarf becomes a constructed dress, with ballet supplying grace of gesture rather than a costume. Outerwear over a performance body is the picture: rehearsal, then the street.

**Sub-themes for chapter development:**
**1. Entre Chien et Loup**
One coat has to carry the hour when day cannot be told from night. Sulfur, oxblood, forest green, and iridescent burgundy replace a simple black night. A sheepskin-collared overcoat darkens the look without a second costume. Twilight is calibration, not mythology.

**2. A Zip That Rewrites the Dress**
A zip-front mini in inky blue opens onto a contrasting shirt. Diagonal and full-length zips are the drawing as well as the closure. Ostrich leather becomes the muscle of a belted jumpsuit. Opening the garment changes the hour it belongs to.

**3. The Ride, Made Urban**
Jodhpurs, flat riding boots, an aviator jacket, and a leather trench keep the equestrian line and set it in the city. Glossy lambskin cycling shorts push the same codes harder. Double-breasted blazers and cigarette trousers are the tailored spine under the ride.

**4. The Scarf Becomes a Dress**
The Carré is draped and then constructed into a dress, the Los Angeles chapter of the same season. A leather parka sits over it as if a dancer just left rehearsal. Ballet supplies the skill of the gesture. It does not supply a costume.
