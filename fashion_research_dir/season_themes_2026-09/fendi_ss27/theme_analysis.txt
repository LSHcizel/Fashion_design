**Theme:**
Fendi Spring/Summer 2027 starts one layer closer to the skin. Clothing is a second skin, porous to emotion, and the body keeps the last word on how visible it wants to be.

**Inspiration:**
Maria Grazia Chiuri's show, her second ready-to-wear collection for the house, opened from underwear: bras, slips, lace rompers, and bloomers as the architecture worn before an outfit decides what it is. The invitation carried an e. e. cummings line about liking one's body with another's. Guests watched through a hall of mirrors. The soundtrack and spirit pass through Raffaella Carrà, a theatrical Italian sensuality that can hint at a nightclub and still refuse costume. The house line she has been using, "Less I. More Us.", sits under a shared wardrobe that is intimate rather than declarative.

**Key aspects of the theme:**
**The body as the base:**
Lingerie is not styling on top of a look. It is the foundation. Everything above it can change texture, proportion, and gender because that base stays.

**Visibility on the wearer's terms:**
A plunge-front sheer midi or a going-out dress with loosening straps can be direct. A black polished suit can refuse exposure. Both are choices. Sensuality is not required to mean undress.

**Strength and fragility in one figure:**
The notes allow a body to be strong and fragile, masculine and feminine, sometimes at once. A slender cape coat over slouchy denim, and plush opera coats beside precise suiting, keep that range inside one wardrobe.

**Glamour that still moves:**
Sequined, fringed, and patterned Baguette bags, headscarves, and beaded surfaces add theater. The clothes stay light. Ease is part of the glamour, not the opposite of it.

**Sub-themes for chapter development:**
**1. Underwear as Architecture**
Bras, slips, lace rompers, and bloomers are the foundation, not a layer added for effect. A salmon-pink lace bloomer and bralette can be the look, or the base another garment is built on. The private piece stays readable after the public layer arrives.

**2. Seen, or Not**
A plunge-front sheer midi and a dress with loosening straps choose exposure. A black polished suit refuses it. Both belong to one wearer who sets the terms. The hall-of-mirrors idea stays in the clothes: visibility is a setting, not a default.

**3. Italian Night, Not Costume**
Headscarves, beads, and a sequined or fringed Baguette carry Raffaella Carrà's theatrical shine. A slender cape and day tailoring keep the nightclub from becoming a costume. The wearer can leave the mood. She is not trapped in it.

**4. Less I, More Us**
A Napoleonic cape over slouchy denim, a plush opera coat, and precise suiting let one wardrobe read strong and fragile, masculine and feminine. Ease stays in the weight of the clothes. The shared line is intimate, not a slogan printed on the garment.
