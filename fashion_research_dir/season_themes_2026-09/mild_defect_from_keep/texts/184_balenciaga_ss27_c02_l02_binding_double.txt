One photograph of one woman at one moment, from one camera. Show only what that camera sees on her body. No collage, no flat lay, no product shot, no extra views, no text.

A long shirt-dress cut with a trailing back panel is the identifying idea, its crisp front staying weekday-straight while the body extends into a quiet evening tail that reads as part of the shirt itself. The dress is optical white poplin with a clean point collar, narrow placket, and long sleeves finished simply at the wrist, then released into a dramatically lengthened back hem that drifts behind the legs without looking detached. Over it sits a small cropped blazer in washed black compact tailoring, worn open just enough to frame the shirt-dress rather than replace it, its shortened body stopping high on the hip so the trailing shirt remains the event. Under the front opening, the dress keeps its straight column and easy waistless line, with a slight side seam pull that lets the fabric fall light and exact. The lower edge shows slim dark trousers beneath the front length, keeping the look street-readable, while pointed black flats stay minimal and close to the ground. A single soft leather bag and plain earrings keep the emphasis on the shirt’s extended hemline. Quiet, precise, mobile. The same jacket is double-breasted with two columns of buttons.
