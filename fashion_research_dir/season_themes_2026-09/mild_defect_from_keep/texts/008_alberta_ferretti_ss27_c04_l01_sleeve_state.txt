A bias slip dress worn open under a sheer robe-coat defines the look as a public inner layer framed by an outer that stays deliberately unfastened, so the opening itself becomes the garment’s identity. The robe-coat falls long and loose in washed cobalt organza with a soft, drifting front edge, its neckline relaxed and its sleeves floating away from the arm to keep the body exposed in motion. Through the open front, a close ivory or blackened-ink bias slip is fully legible on the body, skimming the torso and dropping in a clean liquid line that reads like lingerie made public; fine lace edging or a narrow satin binding may trace the slip neckline and strap line without interrupting its simplicity. The coat’s hem hangs unevenly and slightly longer at one side, with feather-light fraying or a whisper of fringe catching the air, while the inner dress remains unbroken and vertical beneath it. The palette should feel sun-faded yet saturated, with teal and cobalt turning cooler against the pale slip, and the whole silhouette should read as a threshold between private and public rather than a fixed evening gown. Delicate sandals in dark satin or softened metallic leather keep the line light and wearable, with minimal jewelry. A long set-in sleeve covers one arm to the wrist. The other side is sleeveless.
