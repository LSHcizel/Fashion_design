An open robe-coat left deliberately undone so the same-theme inner reads as part of the look: the outer frame is a long, kimono-easy coat that falls in a soft diagonal from shoulder to opposite hip, and inside that opening a narrow satin slip column remains visibly public rather than hidden. The coat is cut in washed sea-glass blue silk with a brushed, light-catching surface that shifts unevenly across the body, and its front edge and hem are traced with a fine, feathered trim that follows the wrap line as a single release path. The coat hangs loose over the torso, its front panels parting to show the inner in the same faded teal family, with a smooth V at the neckline and a slim vertical fall to the hem. The lower edge breaks slightly at one side, letting the opening drift longer than the other and keeping the line of motion legible. Underneath, the slip stays narrow and liquid, skimming the body without stiffness. Delicate sandal straps remain minimal and near invisible, so the eye stays on the opening, the overlap, and the air between layers; the effect is quietly unfastened. The same shell is liquid black satin.
