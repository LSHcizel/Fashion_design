One photograph of one woman at one moment, from one camera. Show only what that camera sees on her body. No collage, no flat lay, no product shot, no extra views, no text.

A long utility coat whose identity is a single fluorescent repair line tracing the whole front opening, lapel edge, and hem like a neon scar, turning the silhouette into one continuous wound-mark. The coat is cut in dark washed indigo denim with a straight, workmanlike column and a hard shoulder, but the acid chartreuse topstitching and tape-like bind along the opening makes the closure path the main graphic event. It is worn open over a cutaway denim bodysuit in soot-black wash, sliced low at the ribs and upper chest so the body shows through one controlled central void, with frayed edges and a rubbed, ghosted monogram field fading across the torso like worn-in residue rather than print. Narrow shredded cargo denim trousers sit close through the hips and thighs, with emptied pocket mouths and raw abrasion at one knee, continuing the damaged language without competing with the coat. Blackened gunmetal hardware, a few dull snaps, and dark boots keep the finish industrial and nocturnal, while the neon seam reads as emergency mending under club light. A long set-in sleeve covers one arm to the wrist. The other side is sleeveless.
