A long black coat-dress whose identity is a single squared window opening at the chest, its crisp frame making the garment read like a facade turned toward light; the cut-out is the look’s dominant feature, not a decoration. The coat falls in a straight, disciplined column to mid-calf, in compact black tweed that suggests a dry surface with subtle grain, and a narrow braid traces the neckline, the front edge, and the window frame in one continuous contour. The body of the coat stays closed and architectural while that opening reveals a controlled inner plane: a fitted black slip-like underlayer with a clean vertical line and a restrained marinière stripe panel in salt white and ink peeking only inside the window, as if the interior room has been brought to the shore. Sleeves are long and plain, ending in neat cuffs; the hem is straight with a slight weight, allowing the silhouette to stand upright. Simple black leather pumps keep the line exact, and small pearl earrings sit close to the ear, almost structural in their restraint. The lower half is a black nylon skirt or a grey wool trouser.
