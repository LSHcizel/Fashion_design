**Theme:**
Louis Vuitton Fall/Winter 2026 is Super Nature. Nicolas Ghesquière treats nature as the first designer and folklore as the attempt to explain wind, rain, snow, and sun. The wardrobe is an anthropology of clothes shaped by altitude and the need to keep moving.

**Inspiration:**
The show closed Paris Fashion Week in the Cour Carrée of the Louvre. Jeremy Hindle, the production designer of Severance, built a geometric, mossy range of prisms and peaks, a pastoral painting inside a science-fiction fable. Ghesquière looked for what mountain people from the Alps to Central Asia to the Andes share: protection, endurance, and freedom of movement. Shaggy capes, cowbells, shearling caps, walking sticks hung with bags, and muddy felt sit beside monumental shoulders and cone-shaped hats. Craft meets technology. Buttons look like minerals, heels like antlers, leather is grooved to resemble wood, invented leather flowers protect as well as decorate, and trunk nail-heads travel onto jewelry. The Noé shifts through time with the house's nomadic bags.

**Key aspects of the theme:**
**Clothes formed by weather:**
Silhouette comes from exposure. Shoulders enlarge, layers wrap, headgear rises. The garment remembers wind and cold even when the material is invented.

**Folklore as a common language:**
Capes, bells, felt, and animalier woven into canvas and denim are not a single national costume. They are the shared answer of people who live high and have to move.

**Nature, seen as if new:**
The set is a landscape everyone knows, rebuilt until it looks alien. Vegetal furs, mineral buttons, and wood-grained leather do the same job in the clothes. Recognition and surprise stay together.

**Travel hardware, still Vuitton:**
The trunk's nail-heads, the Noé, and bags carried on walking sticks keep the house's journey inside the folklore. Nomadism is the brand subject, not a backdrop.

**Sub-themes for chapter development:**
**1. Dressed by the Weather**
Capes and coats take their shape from wind, cold, and altitude. Monumental shoulders, fur epaulettes, and a cone-shaped hat turn protection into architecture. The garment should still read as clothing someone could walk in, inside Ghesquière's science-fiction landscape.

**2. One Mountain, Many Places**
Shearling, muddy felt, a cowbell, and a wrapped cape meet without assigning the look to one country. The Alps, Central Asia, and the Andes share the climate, not a costume. Animalier woven into canvas and denim is the common imprint.

**3. A Natural Material, Invented**
Leather is grooved to mirror wood. Fur reads as plant. Buttons look like minerals, heels like antlers, and leather flowers both decorate and protect. The surface is recognizable and slightly impossible, craft melded with technology.

**4. The Trunk Goes Walking**
Nail-heads from a Louis Vuitton trunk move onto jewelry, beside a Man Ray-like jewel and a Noé carried for a crossing. A walking stick hung with a bag makes the nomadism literal. Travel hardware is the proof, not a logo placed at the end.
