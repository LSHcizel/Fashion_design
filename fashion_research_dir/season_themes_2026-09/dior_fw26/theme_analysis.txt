**Theme:**
Dior Fall/Winter 2026 puts Jonathan Anderson in the garden. A walk in the Tuileries becomes a performance, and the house's giant past is the place the new collection has to start.

**Inspiration:**
The show was staged in the Jardin des Tuileries behind planes of glass. Anderson reworks the Bar jacket, the New Look waist, Monsieur Dior's gray, Marc Bohan's monogram, and the Book Tote, while florals, water-lily shoes, and a green suede turtle bag let nature and artifice trade places. The opening looks stack tutu skirts and frilled trains under three different jackets. Later, Belle Époque ruffles shrink into mini dresses with bouncing trains, bourgeois tweed and gold-button blazers appear beside shearling with a wave hem, and evening coats are worn slouchy, the way a Parisian woman would wear them over the city. Printed silk is asked to look wet, as if the garden's water were still on the cloth.

**Key aspects of the theme:**
**The archive, walked:**
The Bar jacket returns with a mandarin collar and rows of miniature buttons, or cropped and double-breasted over ruffles. Waists cinch. Hips swell with lace or loose pleats of wool and silk. Heritage is a pattern to cut again, not a tableau.

**Garden and city in one day:**
Lily pads, polka dots, crystallized denim, and water-lily shoes keep the park playful. Heavy evening coats, a worn-in jean, and a great blazer keep the same woman in Paris after dark and at the office.

**Real flowers, unreal cloth:**
Florals reflect the set. A print can look like light on water. A skirt can move like a plant. The unreal is allowed once it is tied to something the eye already saw in the garden.

**Levity inside a serious house:**
Bouncing trains, a turtle bag, and wave-cut shearling stop the heritage from becoming solemn. Anderson's Dior can be pretty and still be precise.

**Sub-themes for chapter development:**
**1. The Bar Jacket, Cut Again**
The Bar returns with a mandarin collar and rows of miniature buttons, or cropped and double-breasted over a mass of hem ruffles. The New Look waist stays cinched, and the hip swells in lace or in loose wool and silk pleats. Recutting the archive is the risk. Losing the Dior line is the failure.

**2. A Walk That Becomes a Show**
Water-lily shoes, a floral organza jacket, and a slouchy evening coat are two hours of one Paris day, garden then night. A blazer with a worn-in jean keeps the office in the same passage. The Tuileries set should be felt in the clothes, not only in the room.

**3. Cloth That Holds Water**
Printed silk is asked to look wet, as if the garden's water were still on it. A feather-printed brocade and a skirt that moves like a plant carry the unreal. Tutu layers and frilled trains open the idea. The effect has to stay in the material.

**4. Bourgeois, Then Playful**
Gray suiting, tweed, and gold-button blazers are interrupted by a mini dress with a bouncing train, polka dots, crystallized denim, or a wave-cut shearling. A green suede turtle bag and a scaled Book Tote can carry the joke. The play sits on a correct Dior shoulder.
