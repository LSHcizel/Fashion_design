Heritage & provenance:
- Gabrielle Chanel opened her first couture house at Villa Larralde in Biarritz in 1915. The city is where a new female independence, seaside leisure, and social mobility entered the house.
- The meeting with Ernest Beaux and the N°5 lineage. The 1926 black dress as a radical modern classic, here reconsidered through a bow.
- Working-woman dress turned into luxury: marinière stripes, bleu de travail, washed canvas.

Show / location context:
- Cruise 2027, Matthieu Blazy's first Cruise show for Chanel, at Le Casino Municipal in Biarritz. The runway returns to the coastal city rather than treating it as a backdrop.

Creative director intent:
- Liberation as movement between couture and workwear, black dress and swim, structure and ease.
- Heritage is a living laboratory. Classics were once disruptive, then polished until the rebellion looked inevitable.
- The Atlantic is a design source: rhythm, repetition, and touch, not a holiday postcard.

Silhouette & garment vocabulary:
- Structured jackets and black dresses that can leave a salon. Swim and elevated workwear in the same passage.
- Striped seaside graphics, worker-jacket cuts, and architectural detail. A garment should be credible indoors, on a casino terrace, and nearer the water without a costume change.

Materials, craft & surface:
- Tweed, raffia, silk, beaded knit, sequin, canvas. Softness beside precision. Marine shimmer. The Double C reworked as a structural element, not only a plaque.

Color, stripe & graphic codes:
- Black, Basque stripes, marine and shell tones. Stripes operate as architecture and rhythm.

Accessories & finishing:
- Tools for crossing settings: a striped beach panier, bags with waterproof flaps, Art Deco heels, barefoot heel plates, shell earrings, pearls.
- The accessory should make the change of place look elegant.

Mood, liberation & wearer fantasy:
- A woman who owns her pace and does not change costume when the room opens onto the sea. Freedom is wearable. Rebellion has been refined into a classic and should still feel slightly improper.
