**Theme:**
Chanel Cruise 2027, Sous le Salon la Plage, is a return to Biarritz and a release from the salon toward the sea. Couture discipline stays. The clothes are asked to move between a black dress and swim, between structure and ease.

**Inspiration:**
Matthieu Blazy's first Cruise show for the house was staged at Le Casino Municipal in Biarritz, where Gabrielle Chanel opened her first couture house at Villa Larralde in 1915. The collection uses that origin as a working laboratory: independence, seaside leisure, and the early decision to turn working dress into luxury. It also passes through the meeting with Ernest Beaux and the N°5 story, and through the 1926 black dress, here reconsidered with a bow, as a classic that began as a disruption. Striped seaside graphics, elevated workwear, swim, and architectural detail make the Atlantic a design source rather than a postcard.

**Key aspects of the theme:**
**Origin as a place of motion:**
Biarritz is not scenery. It is where the house learned to make modernity by letting women move. Heritage in this collection is a reason to loosen a code, not a reason to freeze it.

**Salon to shore:**
Interior elegance and outdoor freedom are one wardrobe. A look should be credible in a grand room, on a casino terrace, and nearer the water, without a costume change.

**Workwear, re-authored:**
Marinière stripes, bleu de travail, and washed canvas keep their practical cut and receive Chanel craft. Utility is the modern half of the house, not a contrast stuck on for theme.

**Classics that were once rude:**
The black dress, the bow, and the Double C used as structure remind the collection that Chanel's signatures started as refusals. Rebellion is polished until it looks inevitable.

**Coast without costume:**
Basque stripes, marine shimmer, shell, raffia, tweed, silk, beads, and sequins evoke the Atlantic by rhythm and touch. Bags and shoes, from a striped beach panier to an Art Deco heel, are tools for crossing from one setting to another.

**Sub-themes for chapter development:**
**1. Biarritz as the First Laboratory**
Black, Basque stripes, and a Double C used as structure recall 1915 without staging a period scene. The jacket or dress should feel recently unbound, as if the house were still inventing modernity by the Atlantic. Heritage here is a reason to move.

**2. The Wardrobe That Crosses the Shore**
Marinière stripes, bleu de travail, washed canvas, and swim share a passage with salon tweed. One look has to be credible in a grand room, on the casino terrace, and nearer the water. There is no costume change between those three.

**3. The 1926 Dress, Still Disruptive**
The black dress returns with a bow, or with an equally sharp Chanel interruption. Tweed, silk, and a precise shoulder keep the discipline. The classic should still look slightly improper, because it began as a refusal.

**4. Tools for the Crossing**
A striped beach panier, a bag with a waterproof flap, an Art Deco heel or a barefoot heel plate, shell earrings, and pearls make the change of place. Raffia, beaded knit, and sequin carry the marine touch. The accessory is a tool for the crossing, not a holiday souvenir.
